# Heartform

Six real hearts, each analysed over five minutes and turned into a luminous 3D body and a granular drone built from that person's own ECG.

## The idea
Heart rhythm alone sounds alike in most healthy people. What makes a heart individual is subtler: the shape of each beat seen from two directions, the exact timing of its waves, how the rate swings with every breath and the balance of the nervous system behind it. Heartform measures those things precisely and lets them, and only them, decide what you see and hear.

## What is measured (per person, 5 minutes, two leads)
`extract.py` does the analysis in Python (wfdb, NeuroKit2, SciPy):

| Measurement | Method |
|---|---|
| Heart rate, SDNN, RMSSD, pNN50 | Time-domain HRV from expert-labelled beats, R peaks snapped to the local maximum |
| LF, HF power, LF/HF | Frequency-domain HRV (NeuroKit2) |
| Breathing rate, twice | (1) peak of the HF band of the RR series (respiratory sinus arrhythmia); (2) ECG-derived respiration from R-wave height, since breathing moves the heart in the chest |
| PR, QRS, QT, QTc (Bazett, Fridericia) | Own delineation on the median beat: flattest segment as baseline, slope thresholds for QRS onset and J point, **tangent method** for T end |
| P, R, S, T amplitude, ST level | From the median beat |
| Beat-shape stability | Correlation of every beat with the median beat |
| 40 beats, both leads | For the 3D form |
| 12 s of lead II | The sound source |

## How the data becomes sound
A granular drone. No drums, no melody.

| From the heart | Into the sound |
|---|---|
| Heart rate (e.g. 59.7 bpm = 0.995 Hz) | The ECG is sped up 64× (or 128×) until one heartbeat is one period of a tone: **63.7 Hz**. The person's own ECG waveform is the timbre. |
| RMSSD | Grain density (12 + RMSSD/3 grains per second) |
| QRS duration | Grain length (1.5 × QRS) |
| LF/HF | Pitch set as just-intonation ratios: open fifths (vagal) → fourths → added seconds → semitones and tritone (sympathetic) |
| Beat-shape stability | Detuning of the grains |
| SDNN | Stereo width |
| Breathing rate and HF power | A filter opens and closes at the person's breathing rate; HF sets its depth |
| Real RR series | Exact timing of each heartbeat event |
| PR · QRS · QT · T peak | Each heartbeat is a three-part event: a high **P shimmer** one PR interval early, a low **pressure bloom** with a QRS-long attack and QT-long decay, and a soft **T swell** at the T peak |
| P and T height | Loudness of shimmer and swell |

## How the data becomes an image
Two leads see the heart's electrical activity from two directions. Plotted together, a beat traces a loop (the idea of the vectorcardiogram). Each of the 40 beats is wrapped around a ring, with lead II as height and the second lead as radius. Every ring is turned a little further, so the QRS spikes stand around the axis like a crown. The spikes rise and fall with the R wave, which changes with breathing. A stable heart gives a tight crown, a variable one a frayed crown. The current beat lights up and a comet runs around it at the person's real RR interval. The whole body breathes at their measured breathing rate.

## What went wrong, and what I changed
- **NeuroKit2's wavelet delineation gave impossible values** on these 1970s recordings (QRS up to 244 ms, PR 67 ms, or no result at all). I replaced it with my own transparent delineation on the median beat, where noise averages out, and checked every result by plotting the marks on the beat.
- **P onset in record 123** first ran too far back because the baseline before the P wave was raised (PR 319 ms). It now also stops at the low point before the P wave (PR 253 ms, a genuinely long PR).
- **Flat T waves** make the tangent method unreliable. QT values from a T wave under 0.15 mV are marked *low confidence* (record 122) instead of being hidden.
- **The two breathing estimates** agree within 2 breaths/min in 4 of 6 people. Where they don't, the RSA estimate is used and the disagreement is shown.
- **Units:** LF and HF were first multiplied by 10⁶ twice over. NeuroKit2 already returns ms².

## Running it
Open `index.html`. To regenerate the data:
```
pip install -r requirements.txt
python extract.py path/to/mitdb      # writes data.js
```

## Limits
Five minutes from a 1970s database: two leads, 360 Hz, some noise, patients rather than healthy volunteers. The second lead differs between people (V1, V2 or V5), which changes the loop shape. LF/HF over five minutes is a rough indicator at best. **This is an art and learning piece, not a medical assessment.**
